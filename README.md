## Interaction

`/` opens the terminal. Commands: `help`, `about`, `projects`, `work`, `now`, `contact`, `status`, `lore`, `theme`, `ask`, `email`, `open <project>`, `clear`, `close`. The project list and live links follow the cards on the page. Try `open receipts` or `open feedcairn`. Suggested commands appear as you type. Tab completes unambiguous commands and project names, arrows recall session history while preserving your unfinished input, and Escape closes dialogs. The `projects` result has clickable notes. `clear` removes this terminal’s output and history.

Terminal, Ask khonsu and Email are tabs in one panel frame, so the frame and tabs stay in place when switching. Switching keeps the current conversation and email draft in their separate panels, and keyboard focus lands on the matching tab. The command field stays visible while results scroll. `help` is an aligned command table in every supported language; command words stay untranslated. Touch screens hide the keyboard-only hint.

Ordinary navigation works throughout. Compact project cards (three, two or one per row) show a small lunar tile, the title, a tagline and a one-line description. Each card has on-page **Notes →** and, where one exists, one outside link: a filled pill for a hosted demo or playable build, an outlined pill for source, a download page or a playlist. The tech line and the buttons line up across each row. Across the site ↗ leaves the site and → stays on the page. Search by name, technology or topic, together with the tools, games and earlier-work filters. Search is local and is not stored or sent anywhere.

The header stays at the top of the window: clear over the photo, then frosted glass once the page scrolls under it, with a small moon under the link of the section in view. On phones the section links fold away while scrolling and come back at the top or on keyboard focus. Each section label carries a moon that waxes down the page, from a thin crescent at the field notes to a full moon at Contact. With a mouse, a soft light follows the pointer across a project card. On phones, project notes rise as a sheet from the bottom edge.

Cards open keyboard-accessible notes. The notes put their outside links (demo, playlist or itch.io page, public source, Android preview) above the longer text. “Copy project link” gives a direct address such as https://khns.dev/#project/feedcairn. It opens those notes on arrival. If clipboard access is unavailable, the address remains selectable. Shared links exclude query parameters.

When motion is allowed: dialogs and tool panels fade in and out with their backdrop (discrete transitions where supported, a simple entrance elsewhere), switching Terminal / Ask khonsu / Email keeps the frame still and only fades the content, filter chips morph the project grid with View Transitions where supported (typing in search stays instant), the About and case-study accordions grow by height while their content settles in, replay steps slide a few pixels in their direction, terminal and guide output fades in, buttons press slightly, and cards lift 2 px on hover. The page behind an open dialog no longer scrolls. Motion Off, the system reduced-motion setting (unless Motion is On) and hidden tabs switch everything instantly.

Finite entrance and reveal transitions. Hover effects use transform and a fading shadow layer on devices that support hover. Field notes, skill cards and evidence links use the same visual controls as the rest of the site. The theme switch is described under Appearance. Web Animations used by the replay are cancelled on the next step, on Motion Off and when the tab is hidden. Spotify progress uses a scale transform instead of changing layout width. No animation loop, canvas, video, WebGL, page-scroll handler or third-party font. Visual viewport events keep the terminal and email panel within the available screen height. Reveals stop observing after their first appearance. Hidden-tab motion is disabled. Motion has three saved choices: System follows the OS, On explicitly enables the small transitions, and Off disables them. Preferences are stored for this site in the visitor’s browser. Terminal output and history are bounded.

## Listening now (Spotify)

`api/now-playing.js` is a Vercel Node function that asks Spotify what is playing and returns only the track or podcast episode title, artists or show name, its `open.spotify.com` link, and the track's duration and playback position. It never returns history, artwork, device, playlist or context, and it refuses tokens with anything beyond read-only playback scopes. A null, missing, negative, string or non-finite position is sent as `null` (never 0) and a position past the end is clamped. Playing and idle answers are cached on the CDN for 5 seconds (up to 5 minutes when rate-limited or unconfigured), so visitors share one Spotify request per interval. `now-playing.js` keeps a quiet Spotify summary in On my desk. The summary, the visitor player and the public night playlist share one card beside the note about who Patrick follows (below it on narrower screens). It says "Listening now" with the track only while a track is confirmed playing and fresh, otherwise "Offline", "Updating…" (between songs) or "Currently unavailable"; paused, private session, ads, local files, errors and anything older than the track's end or 150 seconds never show as playing. Before the first answer the status stays blank rather than guessing. The summary keeps the same two rows in every state (the status takes the place of the bar). Between songs ("Updating…") the first row also keeps the height it had while playing, so a title that wraps on a phone does not collapse and grow back. The page below moves only when the next title needs a different number of lines, or on a phone when a two-line title gives way to "Offline". The status is not a live region, so ambient updates never interrupt a screen reader. The page polls every 10 seconds while playing and every 15 seconds otherwise, only while the tab is visible. A request that has not settled after 8 seconds is abandoned; a failed check keeps a still-fresh track and retries at the idle pace.

Below the line, a thin read-only bar shows elapsed and total time, like a phone's mini player. It is estimated on the page: the answer's position plus the CDN `Age` header plus half the request's round trip, counted on the monotonic clock (`performance.now`), never the visitor's wall clock. It steps once a second without transition and only while the tab is visible, clamps at the total, and disappears when the last answer is more than 90 seconds old, so it never runs on forever. A pause or seek between checks is only seen at the next check (every 10 seconds while playing), so the position is approximate; the bar's tooltip and accessible value say so. At the track's expected end the page checks once for the next track; a stale CDN copy of the same track cannot trigger more checks. Hidden tabs keep no timers and make no requests.

"Listen here" loads Spotify's official track embed (`https://open.spotify.com/embed/track/<id>`, or the corresponding episode URL, built only from a validated ID) after a click. Nothing is requested from Spotify before that click, and the frame has no autoplay permission, so nothing plays until the visitor presses play inside it. Spotify decides per visitor whether that is a preview or the full track. The player is separate from Patrick's live line: polls never reload or switch it, and it stays open if Patrick stops. The button is a disclosure toggle ("Listen here" / "Close player", with a stop square while open); reopening loads the track that is playing then, or the last one the visitor opened. "Close player" removes the frame, which stops playback, and returns focus. Below 360px the toggle is a round icon button so the bar keeps its row; its label stays available to assistive technology. "Open in Spotify" stays available. The Content Security Policy allows frames only from `https://open.spotify.com`.

Credentials live only in Vercel environment variables: `SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID`, `SPOTIFY_CLIENT_SECRET` and `SPOTIFY_REFRESH_TOKEN` (authorised with `user-read-currently-playing` only). To create the token, run `SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID=… SPOTIFY_CLIENT_SECRET=… node scripts/spotify-now-playing-token.mjs` on your Mac; it opens Spotify's consent page, copies the refresh token to the clipboard without printing it, and is excluded from deployment. The redirect URI `http://127.0.0.1:8888/callback` must be listed in the Spotify app's settings. Add the three variables to the Production environment and redeploy. The local playlist tool (`com.khonsu.playlists`) keeps its own separate authorisation.

## Ask khonsu guide

guide.js answers through the optional AI endpoint (see Optional AI guide) and keeps a rule-based guide with prepared answers in every language for when AI is not configured or a request fails. No model downloads, tracking, or persisted chat history. The language selector translates the guide interface and chooses the reply language. Only the language preference is saved locally. Supports project dialogs and section navigation, caps the visible log at 30 messages, and renders every message as plain text with textContent. Unknown questions receive a fallback. Edit the topic records when public profile facts change.

Chat interface: a waiting line (a still crescent, shown only after 300 ms) marks a pending reply and is announced through a status region. Replies keep their paragraphs; light Markdown markers from the model are removed, never rendered. Long replies open at their first line. While a reply is pending, send and the suggestions rest (`aria-disabled`, so focus stays), the field stays editable, and Clear cancels. A follow-up sent within five seconds waits out the server's per-visitor spacing, and one `Retry-After` 429 is retried once; other failures show a labelled prepared answer and keep the recent context.

## Optional AI guide

`api/chat.js` uses Groq's `openai/gpt-oss-20b` only for a submitted question, a chosen project-autocomplete result, or an explicit request to explain a comparison. Typing and filtering projects stay in the browser and make no model calls. Add `GROQ_API_KEY` to Vercel Production and redeploy to enable it. Keep that Groq account on the Free plan, without a billing upgrade. The provider quota is shared by all visitors. No paid fallback is configured. Vercel Hobby usage limits still apply.

Without the key, the existing local guide works unchanged. With the key, the panel discloses Groq processing in all 17 languages. It sends the current question and at most two prior exchanges, holds chat history in tab memory, and renders plain text only. Clear cancels an outstanding reply and discards the browser history. Neither endpoint nor client logs or stores chat contents. Groq's own data policy applies: https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data

The server supplies public facts only: the guide topics (except the unverified discovery routine), every project card on the page, the About, practice and On my desk sections in the third person, a description of this site and its features, and the public contact links. For project requests, the model can call one local `recommend_projects` tool. It returns only up to four IDs from the fixed public card list; the page resolves titles, descriptions and links from its own project cards and displays them in a swipeable carousel. “Why these?” explains the public-data basis. Visitors can compare up to three projects in a matrix built from the public card facts: stage and technology, what each project does, and where to try or inspect it. Asking for an AI explanation is optional and sends only those selected public project facts. The autocomplete ranks public project titles, summaries and tags locally; selecting a result asks Groq for a factual project explanation. The guided Agents / RAG / RL path is also optional: after two local choices it sends one focused question through the same endpoint, with prepared answers if AI is unavailable. Model output cannot supply URLs, card copy or new projects. No other tools, account access, email sending or browser access are available to the model. `tests/chat.test.cjs` keeps the project list, terminal commands and contact links in step with the page. The prompt makes the guide speak about Patrick in the third person, never as him, treats "you" questions as questions about Patrick, and tells it to say when the portfolio does not mention something instead of guessing tools, dates or clients. Temperature is 0.2 and replies are capped at 800 completion tokens. The system prompt is about 8,300 characters (roughly 1,950 tokens); a test keeps it under 8,400. Groq's Free plan counts each request's prompt plus its declared maximum output against 8K tokens per minute (and 200K per day), so a burst of more than about three questions a minute across all visitors gets labelled prepared answers until the budget refills. When Groq asks for a wait of 10 seconds or less, the endpoint passes it on as `Retry-After` and the page retries that question once. The server also rejects custom roles and oversized histories, limits output, times out requests, and returns a labelled prepared answer on failures. The warm-instance throttle is best effort, not a distributed abuse or spending cap. Provider Free-plan limits remain essential. Prompt instructions reduce unsupported claims but cannot guarantee factual answers.
